How it worksMeasurements, graphs and image comparisons+

The measurements behind this result

These describe how frames were grouped. They are not an accuracy score.

Where the picture changes

Higher peaks mean a larger visual change between sampled frames. Dotted lines mark detected scene changes.

How the pictures form groups

Each dot is a sampled frame. Its colour shows the group it joined. Nearby dots have similar measured features.

Preparing a picture for comparison

The system compares brightness, texture and edges, rather than understanding the story.

A(C) measures within-cluster association at the reported SL, not scene-detection accuracy. ONM works best with sudden changes between shots. It can miss slow fades or shots that differ mainly in colour.

Behind the demo

Finding scenes by comparing pictures

A video is a sequence of still pictures. Our system compares them, groups similar ones and marks where those groups change.

  1. 1

    Look at the pictures

    Take frames from the video and measure their brightness, texture and edges.

  2. 2

    Find similar groups

    ONM chooses the number of groups from the frames, without being told a number in advance.

  3. 3

    Mark the changes

    Use changes between groups to divide the video into scenes you can jump to.

Best for sudden shot changes. Slow fades and colour-only changes may be missed.
